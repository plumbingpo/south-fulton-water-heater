When to Call for Commercial Water Heaters

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Fixtures that run too hot one minute and cool the next
  • Long waits for hot water at sinks far from the heater
  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most

The engineering behind commercial hot water is more demanding. Large gas inputs require confirming the supply pressure, meter size, combustion air, and vent capacity. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
