Many of the installation calls we get in South Fulton, GA are not replacements at all. A family adds a bathroom and needs more capacity. Someone wants the tank moved from a hall closet to the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. South Fulton Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Every installation starts with sizing and location. Tanks are sized by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at your water's temperature rise, so the heater keeps pace with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. We talk it all through in plain language and then give you a firm price.

The install itself follows a checklist: gas lines sized, run, and leak-tested with a sediment trap at the heater, venting sized and pitched to the manufacturer's spec, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection between copper and steel. A full-size relief valve discharge line runs to a safe point, and any required expansion tank, drain pan, seismic straps, or permit is part of the job. Before we leave, we fill and purge the tank, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the temperature near 120 degrees, and walk you through everything. When to Call for Water Heater Installation

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
