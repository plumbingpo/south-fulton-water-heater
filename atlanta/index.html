Keeping Your Atlanta Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Atlanta

Whatever type of water heater your Atlanta home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
