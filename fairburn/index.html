How Fairburn Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Most water heater problems we see in Fairburn start with what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Code is another piece. Replacing an older heater often means updating the installation along the way: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a T&P discharge line that terminates correctly, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, including extra sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that occasionally shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (470) 201-3612 to set up a visit.
