Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the tank is often leaking. That is the reason maintenance matters for South Fulton, GA homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

A little rust-colored water or a relief valve that drips now and then can be the first hint of a tank that is close to the end. If you notice any of these signs, contact South Fulton Water Heater at (470) 201-3612 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
